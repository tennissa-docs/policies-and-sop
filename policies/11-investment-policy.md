> **Plain-language summary.** TENNISSA holds charitable funds in safe, liquid bank accounts. We do not gamble with scholarship money in the stock market. We prioritize protecting the principal so that funds are always available for students.

---

## 1. Purpose
This policy establishes guidelines for the management and investment of TENNISSA's financial assets. As a Scholarship Granting Organization (SGO), TENNISSA acts as a fiduciary for Section 25F contributions, which must be disbursed to schools within strict timeframes. Therefore, capital preservation and liquidity are the highest priorities.

## 2. Investment Objectives
1. **Preservation of Capital:** The primary objective is to protect the principal value of all funds.
2. **Liquidity:** Funds must be readily available to meet scholarship disbursement schedules and operational expenses without penalty.
3. **Yield:** Achieving a reasonable rate of return is a secondary objective, pursued only after capital preservation and liquidity requirements are met.
4. **Faith-Based Alignment:** Where financially feasible and compliant with fiduciary duties, TENNISSA will seek banking and cash-management solutions that align with Islamic financial principles (e.g., avoiding interest-bearing accounts in favor of permissible profit-sharing or non-interest accounts), provided such accounts offer equivalent safety (FDIC insurance).

## 3. Permitted Investments
Funds may only be held in the following low-risk, highly liquid instruments:
- FDIC-insured checking and savings accounts.
- FDIC-insured Certificates of Deposit (CDs), provided their maturity dates align with scholarship disbursement needs.
- Money Market deposit accounts.
- U.S. Treasury bills or notes with maturities of less than one year.

## 4. Prohibited Investments
To protect charitable assets, TENNISSA shall **not** invest in:
- Equities (stocks) or mutual funds containing equities.
- Corporate bonds or high-yield (junk) bonds.
- Cryptocurrency or digital assets.
- Real estate, commodities, or derivatives.
- Any speculative or illiquid asset.

## 5. Account Segregation
As required by the Financial Controls Policy (FIN-001) and Section 25F regulations, credit-eligible scholarship funds must be maintained in a strictly segregated account. Investment yields or earnings generated by the Section 25F account must remain in that account and be used exclusively for scholarships or permitted administrative allocations.

## 6. Oversight and Reporting
The Financial Controller Director is responsible for managing cash flow and ensuring funds are held in permitted accounts. The status of all accounts, including balances and yields, shall be included in the monthly financial report provided to the Board of Directors.
