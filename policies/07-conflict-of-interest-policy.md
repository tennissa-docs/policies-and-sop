## 1. Purpose

This policy protects TENNISSA's interests whenever it considers a transaction or arrangement that might benefit the private interest of an insider. It is designed to:

- follow the elements of the IRS sample conflict-of-interest policy (Form 1023 instructions) and support TENNISSA's Form 990 disclosures;
- support the rebuttable presumption of reasonableness under IRC § 4958 and Treas. Reg. § 53.4958-6;
- meet the director conflict rules of the Tennessee Nonprofit Corporation Act, Tenn. Code Ann. §§ 48-58-701 to 48-58-704; and
- carry out the Section 25F prohibition on scholarships to disqualified persons.

This policy supplements, and does not replace, any applicable law.

## 2. Covered Persons

This policy applies to ("Covered Persons"):

- all directors, **including the Founding Directors**, and all officers;
- the Executive Director;
- Scholarship Committee members and scholarship reviewers;
- employees, contractors and volunteers with authority over money, scholarships, school approval or governance; and
- anyone else exercising delegated authority for TENNISSA.

## 3. Definitions

- **Financial interest.** A direct or indirect ownership or investment interest, compensation arrangement, or potential interest or arrangement with TENNISSA or with any person or entity TENNISSA does business with, including a participating school, vendor or donor.
- **Conflict of interest.** A Covered Person's financial, family, employment or other interest could improperly influence, or reasonably appear to influence, their judgment for TENNISSA. A conflict can exist even when no wrongdoing has occurred.
- **Family member (for this policy generally).** A spouse; ancestors; siblings (including half-siblings) and their spouses; children, grandchildren and great-grandchildren and their spouses (following IRC § 4958(f)(4)); household members; and any other person whose interests could reasonably affect the Covered Person's judgment.
- **Section 25F family member.** For the scholarship bar in §4.1, the broader Section 25F definition applies: spouses; ancestors and descendants of the individual **or the individual's spouse**; siblings of the individual or the individual's spouse; **descendants of those siblings** (nieces, nephews and their children); and the spouse of any of these. Descendants include adopted children and stepchildren.
- **Disqualified person (Section 25F).** Directors, officers, anyone who participates in selecting scholarship recipients, substantial contributors, and their Section 25F family members. See [SCH-001 §5.6](01-scholarship-policy.md).
- **Related entity.** A business, school or organization in which a Covered Person or family member holds more than a 10% interest or serves as a director, officer, trustee, employee or key decision maker.
- **Qualified (disinterested) director.** A director who has no conflict in the matter and is not a family member of, or in a business or employment relationship with, the interested person.

## 4. Conflicts That Cannot Be Cured by Recusal

The following are **prohibited**. Disclosure and recusal do not make them permissible:

1. **Scholarships to disqualified persons.** No scholarship may be awarded to a disqualified person, including any Section 25F family member of a director, officer, selection participant or substantial contributor.
2. **Loans to insiders.** TENNISSA may not lend money to, or guarantee a debt of, any director, officer or employee.
3. **Revenue-based insider pay.** No Covered Person may be paid a percentage of, or by a formula based on, contributions, scholarship volume or revenue.
4. **Self-approval.** No one may approve their own payment, reimbursement, contract or other benefit, or one for a family member.
5. **Donor influence for benefit.** No Covered Person may arrange or accept a gift to TENNISSA in exchange for a scholarship, a school benefit or any other private benefit.

## 5. Duty to Disclose; Annual Statement; Registers

1. A Covered Person discloses any actual, potential or apparent conflict, with all material facts, **before** the matter is discussed or decided. Where possible the disclosure is in writing to the Board Chair, or to a disinterested director if the Chair is involved.
2. Each Covered Person completes the **annual statement** (Appendix A) when they join and every January, and updates it within 30 days of any change.
3. **Standing disclosures** include:
   - any employment by, board service with, ownership of, or tuition-paying relationship with a participating or prospective school;
   - any school-age relative (using the Section 25F family definition) who may apply for a scholarship;
   - any financial interest in a vendor, contractor or donor that does business with TENNISSA; and
   - any compensation or payments from TENNISSA or a related entity.
4. The Board's designee keeps two confidential registers:
   - a **conflicts register** of disclosed interests and how each was handled; and
   - a **disqualified-person register** of the names of disqualified persons and their known Section 25F family members, updated each January and whenever a selection participant or substantial contributor is added. Scholarship staff check every application against it.

## 6. Determining Whether a Conflict Exists

After disclosure and any discussion with the Board, the interested person leaves the meeting while the qualified directors decide whether a conflict exists. The interested person may answer factual questions before leaving.

## 7. Recusal and Approval Procedure

1. The interested person may present factual information, then leaves the meeting during discussion and the vote, and may not try to influence the outcome indirectly.
2. If appropriate, the qualified directors may appoint a disinterested person or committee to investigate alternatives.
3. After due diligence, the qualified directors decide whether TENNISSA can obtain, with reasonable effort, a more advantageous arrangement that does not involve a conflict.
4. If not, they decide whether the transaction is in TENNISSA's best interest, for its own benefit, and fair and reasonable, and approve or reject it.
5. **Tennessee voting rule.** Approval requires the affirmative vote of a majority of the qualified directors voting, and **at least two** qualified directors must vote in favor (Tenn. Code Ann. § 48-58-703). If fewer than two qualified directors are available, the transaction may not proceed unless counsel confirms another lawful route under § 48-58-702.

## 8. Board Composition

1. Because the three Founding Directors hold permanent seats and one is the Executive Director, a matter involving the founders may leave **fewer than two** qualified directors. TENNISSA therefore shall seat at least **two independent directors** no later than January 1, 2027. An independent director is not an employee, not a family member of a director or officer, and has no material business relationship with TENNISSA, a director, an officer or a participating school.
2. At least one independent director sits on any committee that sets compensation or oversees the audit.
3. Until independent directors are seated, conflict matters involving the founders (including founder compensation) are decided only after written advice from independent counsel or a CPA, and only by a lawful vote under §7.5.


## 9. Scholarship Applicants, Recipients and Participating Schools

1. A Covered Person discloses any personal, family, financial or employment relationship with a scholarship applicant and may not review, score, recommend, approve or administer that application.
2. Disqualified persons are **ineligible** for scholarships (§4.1).
3. A Covered Person employed by, serving on the board of, holding a financial interest in, or paying tuition to a school recuses from:
   - approving, reverifying or suspending that school;
   - setting rules that would favor that school; and
   - deciding applications from students at that school.
4. A conflict never waives the school requirements in [SCH-001 §4](01-scholarship-policy.md).
5. Scholarship recipients and their families may not be offered or given anything of value by a Covered Person in connection with an award, and may not be asked for anything in return.

## 10. Other Assistance Coordination

A conflicted person may not determine or influence the calculation of Other Assistance or Remaining Need under [SCH-001 §7](01-scholarship-policy.md) for a student with whom they have a relationship.

## 11. Compensation of Insiders

1. The Executive Director, and any other director or officer who is paid, may not take part in deliberating or voting on their own compensation, benefits, reimbursements or employment terms, or those of a family member.
2. Compensation is approved **in advance** by a body made up entirely of qualified directors (§7.5). To qualify for the § 4958 rebuttable presumption, that body:
   - (a) relies on appropriate **comparability data**, such as pay at similarly sized nonprofits, compensation surveys or written offers;
   - (b) **documents** the decision at the time: the terms, date, directors present and voting, comparability data relied on, and any conflicts disclosed. Minutes are prepared before the later of the next meeting or 60 days after the decision, and approved within a reasonable time after that; and
   - (c) sets compensation as a **fixed amount** or hourly rate for a stated period, payable from the **operating account** only.
3. Revenue-based or formula-based compensation is prohibited (§4.3).
4. Directors are not paid for serving as directors.

## 12. Related-Party Transactions

1. TENNISSA avoids related-party transactions when a reasonable alternative exists.
2. A necessary related-party transaction is approved under §7, after considering alternatives and market pricing, and is documented under §15.
3. Related-party transactions are reported on Form 990 (including Schedule L) as required.

## 13. Donors, Gifts and Confidential Information

1. A Covered Person discloses any personal benefit or relationship that could affect a decision about accepting a gift or applying a restriction.
2. Covered Persons may not solicit or accept gifts, favors or entertainment worth more than **$100** in a year from any scholarship applicant, participating school, vendor or donor with business before TENNISSA, other than customary courtesy items.
3. Confidential information may not be used for personal benefit or the benefit of a third party.

## 14. Violations

If the qualified directors have reasonable cause to believe a Covered Person failed to disclose a conflict, they tell the person and give them a chance to explain. If a violation is confirmed, TENNISSA may take corrective action, including:

- reconsidering the affected decision where lawful;
- recovering any excess benefit;
- restricting the person's authority;
- removing them from a committee or office; or
- other action consistent with the Bylaws and the law.

For a Founding Director, action is limited to what the Bylaws and the law allow. Removal from a **seat** is available only as permitted there, including judicial removal under the Tennessee Nonprofit Corporation Act.


## 15. Documentation in Board Minutes

Minutes of any meeting involving a conflict record:

- the name of the interested person and the nature of the conflict;
- whether the qualified directors found a conflict;
- confirmation that the person left the meeting and did not vote;
- the names of the qualified directors present and how each voted;
- the alternatives and comparability data considered; and
- the decision and the reasons for it.

## 16. Periodic Review

To ensure TENNISSA operates consistently with its charitable purposes and does not put its tax-exempt status at risk, the Board conducts an **annual review** of:

- whether compensation arrangements are reasonable and based on competent survey information;
- whether arrangements with participating schools, vendors and any joint venture follow written policies, are properly recorded, reflect reasonable payments and further charitable purposes rather than private benefit; and
- whether disqualified-person screening was carried out for every award.

The Board may use outside advisors for this review, but remains responsible for it.

## 17. Administration

The Board designates a disinterested director, or an independent professional, to collect annual statements, keep the registers in §5.4 and report to the Board each year.
