## 1. Purpose

This policy encourages good-faith reports of suspected wrongdoing, protects people who make them, and sets out how reports are investigated and documented. It supports TENNISSA's Form 990 governance disclosures and is consistent with federal and Tennessee anti-retaliation laws, including:

- 18 U.S.C. § 1513(e), which makes it a crime to retaliate against someone for giving truthful information about a possible federal offense to law enforcement; and
- the Tennessee Public Protection Act, Tenn. Code Ann. § 50-1-304, which protects employees who refuse to take part in, or remain silent about, illegal activities.

## 2. Who May Report

Anyone may report: directors, officers, employees, volunteers, scholarship reviewers, contractors, families, participating schools, donors and members of the public.

## 3. What to Report

Report suspected:

- fraud, theft or misuse of scholarship or operating funds;
- improper scholarship selection, manipulation of eligibility or income documentation, or awards to disqualified persons;
- donor earmarking, tuition-swap arrangements or quid-pro-quo gifts;
- undisclosed conflicts of interest or related-party transactions;
- false financial records, donor acknowledgments or regulatory filings (IRS, TDOE, Tennessee Secretary of State);
- violations of Section 25F, other laws, donor restrictions or TENNISSA policies;
- misleading fundraising or solicitation;
- misuse or unauthorized disclosure of student or donor data;
- a participating school misrepresenting enrollment, tuition, aid or accreditation;
- harassment or discrimination (also covered by the [Employment Policy](10-employment-policy.md)); and
- retaliation against anyone who raised a concern.

## 4. How to Report Internally

| If the concern involves… | Report to |
|---|---|
| Anyone other than those below | The Board Chair or the designated Compliance Contact |
| The Board Chair or the Executive Director | Any director who is not involved, or the Independent Reporting Contact |
| A Founding Director, or a majority of the Board | The **Independent Reporting Contact**: outside counsel, a CPA or another independent professional designated by the Board and named on TENNISSA's website |

Reports may be written or oral. A person receiving an oral report writes a short summary and confirms it with the reporter where possible.

**Anonymous internal reports** may be made to the Independent Reporting Contact through Independent Advisory Committee (IAC). Anonymous reports are harder to follow up, so include enough detail (who, what, when, where, and any documents) for an investigation.


## 5. External Reporting

Nothing in this policy limits anyone's right to report directly to a government agency, with or without first reporting internally, and no agreement with TENNISSA may prohibit it. Agencies include:

| Concern | Agency |
|---|---|
| Misleading or improper charitable solicitation or fundraising; failure to register to solicit | **Tennessee Secretary of State, Division of Charitable Solicitations and Gaming** (online nonprofit complaint form at sos.tn.gov/charities, or (615) 741-2555). **Where applicable, anonymous reports may be submitted to this office.** |
| Misuse of charitable assets or breaches of duty by directors or officers | Tennessee Attorney General, Public Interest Division (Public Benefit Nonprofit Complaint Form) |
| Section 25F scholarship program compliance (eligibility, SGO requirements) | Tennessee Department of Education |
| Federal tax-exempt or Section 25F violations | IRS, Form 13909 (Tax-Exempt Organization Complaint/Referral) |
| Crimes | Law enforcement |

## 6. Good Faith

A report must be based on a reasonable belief that wrongdoing may have occurred. You do not need proof. A person who knowingly makes a false report may face corrective action.

## 7. No Retaliation

TENNISSA prohibits retaliation against anyone who, in good faith:

- makes a report, internally or externally;
- takes part in an investigation;
- gives information to a regulator or law enforcement; or
- refuses to take part in an activity they reasonably believe is illegal.

Retaliation includes threats, harassment, dismissal, demotion, reduced hours, loss of volunteer roles, and any adverse action on a scholarship application, a school's participation or a donor relationship. Anyone who retaliates is subject to corrective action, up to removal from their position as permitted by the Bylaws and law. A report of retaliation is itself investigated under §8.

## 8. Investigation Responsibilities

The **Investigation Lead** is the person who received the report under §4, unless that person is implicated, in which case it is the Independent Reporting Contact. The Investigation Lead:

1. **Acknowledges** the report within **5 business days**, if the reporter can be contacted.
2. **Assesses** whether it falls under this policy, decides whether interim steps are needed (for example, suspending a person's financial authority), and issues a legal hold under [REC-001 §6](03-records-retention-policy.md) if needed.
3. **Investigates** promptly and fairly. Counsel, accountants or other independent professionals may be engaged. A person implicated in the report may not control, direct or be informed of the investigation beyond what fairness requires, and is given a chance to respond.
4. **Concludes**, normally within **60 days**, with written findings and recommendations. If more time is needed, the Investigation Lead tells the qualified directors why.
5. **Reports** to the qualified (disinterested) directors in summary at the next meeting. Serious matters are reported immediately. Where appropriate, the reporter is told that the matter has been addressed.

## 9. Documentation

For every report, the Investigation Lead keeps a confidential file containing:

- the date and channel of the report and a summary of the concern;
- the acknowledgment and any interim steps;
- the evidence gathered and people interviewed;
- the written findings, decision and corrective action; and
- the date the matter was closed and any notice to the reporter or authorities.

A confidential log of all reports (date, category, status and outcome, without the reporter's identity) is kept by the Independent Reporting Contact and summarized to the Board each year. Files are kept under the [Records Retention Policy](03-records-retention-policy.md).

## 10. Confidentiality

The reporter's identity and the report are shared only with people who need them to investigate or resolve the matter, or as the law requires. TENNISSA will protect confidentiality as far as possible but cannot guarantee it.

## 11. Corrective Action

Corrective action may include:

- recovery of funds;
- correcting donor acknowledgments or government filings;
- added controls;
- discipline or removal (for Founding Directors, only as permitted by the Bylaws and the law);
- suspending a participating school;
- revising policies; and
- notifying authorities where required.

## 12. Distribution and Review

This policy is distributed every year to directors, officers, staff, volunteers and scholarship reviewers, and is posted on TENNISSA's website with the reporting contacts. The Board reviews it every year.
