> **Plain-language summary.** If TENNISSA ever partners with a business or another organization, the arrangement must keep TENNISSA in control of its charitable mission and money, must not benefit any private person beyond fair value, and must be approved by the Board after legal review.

---

## 1. Purpose

This policy protects TENNISSA's tax-exempt status and charitable assets when it participates in a joint venture or similar arrangement with a taxable entity or a related party. It supports TENNISSA's response to IRS Form 990, Part VI, Line 16 and follows IRS guidance on joint ventures, including Rev. Rul. 98-15 and Rev. Rul. 2004-51.

## 2. Definitions

- **Joint venture.** Any joint ownership or contractual arrangement through which TENNISSA and another party jointly undertake a business enterprise, investment or exempt-purpose activity, including partnerships, LLCs, co-branded programs and revenue-sharing arrangements.
- **Related-party arrangement.** Any joint venture or similar arrangement in which a director, officer, substantial contributor, or a family member or related entity of any of them, has a financial interest.
- **Not a joint venture.** Ordinary arm's-length vendor contracts (for example, paying a software provider or auditor) and school participation agreements. These follow [FIN-001](02-financial-controls-policy.md) and, where an insider is involved, the [Conflict of Interest Policy](07-conflict-of-interest-policy.md).

## 3. Required Safeguards

Every joint venture agreement must:

1. **Charitable control.** Give TENNISSA control, or a veto, sufficient to ensure the venture's activities further TENNISSA's charitable and educational purposes.
2. **Priority of charitable purpose.** State that the venture's charitable purposes take priority over maximizing profit for any other party.
3. **No private inurement or excess private benefit.** Prohibit any insider from receiving TENNISSA's net earnings, and limit any benefit to another party to what is incidental and at fair market value.
4. **Arm's-length terms.** Set all payments, capital contributions and allocations at fair market value, supported by documentation.
5. **Financial oversight.** Give TENNISSA the right to receive regular financial reports, to inspect books and records, and to require an annual independent review or audit of the venture.
6. **Exit rights.** Let TENNISSA withdraw, or unwind the arrangement, if it threatens TENNISSA's tax-exempt status or SGO status.

## 4. Section 25F Funds and Scholarship Functions

1. Section 25F funds may never be contributed to, invested in or used by a joint venture.
2. TENNISSA may not share control of scholarship selection, eligibility or award decisions with any other party.
3. No arrangement may pay any party a share of qualified contributions or scholarships, or tie any party's compensation to contribution volume.

## 5. Legal Review and Board Approval

1. Before any proposal goes to the Board, counsel reviews it for tax-exempt status, unrelated business income (IRC §§ 511–514), private benefit, excess benefit (IRC § 4958) and Section 25F effects, and gives written advice.
2. The Board approves any joint venture **in advance**. Approval requires a majority of qualified (disinterested) directors voting, with at least two qualified directors voting in favor, under [COI-001 §7.5](07-conflict-of-interest-policy.md).
3. Interested persons disclose their interests and recuse under the [Conflict of Interest Policy](07-conflict-of-interest-policy.md). The minutes record the matters listed in COI-001 §15, the counsel advice relied on, and how each safeguard in §3 is met.

## 6. Ongoing Monitoring and Reporting

1. The Executive Director reports to the Board at least annually on each active joint venture: activities, finances, compliance with the safeguards, and whether it continues to serve TENNISSA's mission.
2. The Board reviews each joint venture at least annually and acts promptly if a safeguard is not being met.
3. Joint ventures and related-party arrangements are reported on Form 990 (including Schedules L and R) as required.

## 7. Review

The Board reviews this policy every year.
