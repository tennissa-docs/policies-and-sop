## 1. Purpose

This policy sets standards for creating, keeping, protecting and disposing of TENNISSA's records. It supports:

- the general recordkeeping duty in IRC § 6001 and the Section 25F reporting and recordkeeping requirements (proposed Treas. Reg. § 1.25F-4);
- TENNISSA's Form 990 governance disclosures; and
- 18 U.S.C. § 1519, which makes it a federal crime to destroy, alter or conceal records to obstruct a federal investigation. It applies to nonprofits.

## 2. General Requirements

1. TENNISSA keeps records that document its governance, charitable activities, scholarship decisions, finances, donor restrictions and Section 25F compliance.
2. Records may be electronic or paper. They must stay complete, accessible, readable and protected for the full retention period.
3. **Data minimization.** TENNISSA does not collect or keep personal data it does not need (see [Privacy](06-privacy-confidentiality-policy.md) §3).
4. **Official copy.** Where records are kept in TENNISSA's approved scholarship or donation platform, the platform record is the official copy. Vendor contracts must let TENNISSA export its records in a usable format and keep them for the periods below, including after the contract ends. TENNISSA exports a full copy at least annually and before any contract ends.
5. **TENNISSA systems only.** TENNISSA records are kept in TENNISSA-controlled accounts, not personal email or personal cloud storage.

## 3. Access and Confidentiality

1. Access to records is limited to people who need it for their role, under [Privacy](06-privacy-confidentiality-policy.md) §5.
2. Records containing protected information (student, family, donor, financial or personnel information) are stored in access-controlled systems with multi-factor authentication. Paper records are kept in locked storage.
3. The Records Custodian keeps a simple index of where each record category is stored and who has access.
4. Access is removed within **2 business days** after a person's role ends. Departing directors, officers, staff and volunteers return all TENNISSA records and delete copies from personal devices.

Unless a longer period is required by law, contract, grant, donor restriction or a legal hold (§6):

## 5. Public Disclosure

As federal law requires, TENNISSA makes the following available for public inspection and provides copies on request:

- its Form 1023 application and IRS determination letter; and
- its three most recent annual information returns (Form 990 series), excluding the names and addresses of donors.

Donor identities on Schedule B and in Section 25F filings are **not** public. They are protected under the [Privacy Policy](06-privacy-confidentiality-policy.md).

## 6. Preservation and Legal Holds

1. When litigation, an audit, a government inquiry or an investigation is pending or **reasonably anticipated**, the Records Custodian or counsel issues a written legal hold to everyone who may hold relevant records.
2. Routine destruction of affected records stops at once, including automatic deletion settings in email and platforms.
3. The hold lasts until counsel releases it in writing.
4. Anyone who destroys, alters or hides a record covered by a hold, or does so to obstruct an investigation, is subject to removal from their role and may face criminal liability.

## 7. Secure Destruction

1. Records past their retention period and not under a hold are destroyed during an annual review approved by the Records Custodian and one other officer.
2. Paper records with protected information are cross-cut shredded or destroyed by a certified destruction vendor.
3. Electronic records are deleted using methods suited to their sensitivity, including from backups as they cycle. Devices are wiped or physically destroyed before disposal or reuse.
4. A destruction log records the category, date range, method, date and who approved each destruction. No protected information is recorded in the log.
5. Destruction must **never** be used to hide misconduct or interfere with an investigation.

## 8. Responsibilities

The Records Custodian runs this policy under Board oversight, trains people who handle records, and reports on compliance to the Board each year.

## 9. Review

The Board reviews this policy every year and whenever federal regulations, IRS guidance, TDOE rules or Tennessee's state scholarship programs change.
